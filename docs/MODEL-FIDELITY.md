# Models and limits

The Observatory is a set of interactive physics models with idealized geometry and illustrative parameters. Each scene states its model; none is an exact reconstruction of a real object.

The equations can be exact within a chosen model while that model omits real effects. Numerical error, model error and an unmeasured result are different things. The checks below are implementation checks, not scientific certification. See the [developer guide](DEVELOPING.md#unattended-test-runs) for commands; an unavailable or unrun check supplies no accuracy evidence.

## Chaos labs

Three-body forces and the displayed energy use the same softened potential, `U = -sum(i<j) mi*mj/sqrt(rij² + epsilon²)`. The launch nudge changes the cyan body's velocity before subtracting the system's centre-of-mass velocity. It therefore changes relative motion without giving the entire lab a net drift. The figure-eight starts from [Chenciner and Montgomery's equal-mass orbit](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0011268); softening is an explicit modification. The equal-mass rotating triangle uses the circular angular speed for the softened force, and is unstable to perturbations.

The pendulum equations follow the point-mass, massless-rod model [derived by Erik Neumann](https://www.myphysicslab.com/pendulum/double-pendulum-en.html). Its error readout divides energy change by `3mgL`, rather than by initial energy, which can be near zero. The amber and cyan drawings have a tiny depth offset for visibility; physical separation is measured in their common plane.

Both labs retain elapsed-time remainders and advance in fixed steps as frames arrive. Speed changes the number of steps, not their size. Trails sample at 60 points per simulation-time unit with a bounded 16-unit history and survive pausing. The app caps wall-frame time at 0.05 s, so overloaded rendering slows the experience rather than catching up without a work limit. Replay uses the same initial data and no randomness.

Run [the production-model checks](../Tools/check-chaos.ps1) for the fixtures and tolerances in [PhysicsChecks.cs](../Tools/chaos/PhysicsChecks.cs). Passing those checks verifies the stated numerical cases, not every slider value or arbitrarily long trajectories. The Unity editor was unavailable on the implementation machine; rendering, scene input and performance remain unobserved.

## Additional exhibits

These ten exhibits use normalized model units and illustrative drawing scales. Their UI labels and notes distinguish the equations from real-world predictions. `LabScene` advances fixed steps with a carried time remainder; playback speed changes the number of steps. Wall-frame time is capped at 0.05 s, so an overloaded machine slows the model instead of accumulating unlimited work. Replay resets clocks and trails. Seeded random layouts are repeatable within the same runtime. Particle trails retain at most 960 samples (16 model-time units); markers share one mesh.

| Exhibit | Equations and integration | Limits and checks |
|---|---|---|
| Lorenz attractor | `x'=10(y-x)`, `y'=x(rho-z)-y`, `z'=xy-(8/3)z`; RK4, dt=1/240. Twin starts offset along x. | State-space coordinates, not geographic positions or a weather forecast. Equilibrium, finite-interval bounds, timestep refinement and replay checks. |
| Reaction–diffusion | Gray–Scott `u'=0.16 lap(u)-uv²+F(1-u)`, `v'=0.08 lap(v)+uv²-(F+k)v`; 64×64 periodic unit grid, four-neighbour Laplacian, explicit Euler dt=1. At 1×, 128 steps per wall second. | Synthetic chemistry; no biological organism or measured chemical calibration. Presets/corner settings run for 3,000 steps with positivity/bounds checks; periodic transport and the homogeneous equilibrium are tested. Display colours are nonlinear concentration mappings. |
| Wave interference | Scalar `h''=c² lap(h)-0.12h'`; 64×64 unit grid, dt=1/120, velocity-first update with implicit damping. Two cells prescribe sinusoidal displacement and velocity. Walls fix displacement to zero. | Linear field, not Navier–Stokes water. No turbulence, depth or breaking waves. Speed ≤12 gives `c*dt≤0.1`, below the square-grid stability bound. Still water, pulse damping, fixed edges, early midpoint cancellation and driven bounds are tested. |
| Gravity assist | Unit-mass particle in `U=-1/r`, velocity Verlet dt=1/1200. A second inertial frame adds constant planet velocity along +y to the probe velocity. | No Sun or planet backreaction; finite-distance launch. Stops before r<0.08 or after r>40. Energy/angular momentum, mirrored flybys, moving-frame gain/loss and singularity stop are tested. |
| Lagrange-point lab | Planar circular restricted three-body equations in the rotating frame, normalized separation/mass/angular rate, RK4 dt=1/2400. `C=x²+y²+2[(1-mu)/r1+mu/r2]-v²`. | Massless probe; stops within 0.03 of a primary or beyond radius 8. L4/L5 are linearly stable when `27mu(1-mu)<1`; this does not guarantee every finite perturbation stays bounded. Equilibria and 60-time-unit Jacobi checks include both stability regimes. |
| Charged particle | Analytic `v'=q(E+v cross B)`, mass=1, q=±1, E along x, B along z. Draw samples at 1/120 model-time unit. | Nonrelativistic, uniform fields; no radiation, collisions or backreaction. Work-energy and full-cycle `E cross B / B²` drift identities are checked for both signs and field/pitch extremes. |
| Rutherford scattering | Seventeen independent unit-mass projectiles with `U=k/r`, velocity Verlet dt=1/1200. Beam varies impact parameter; initial kinetic energy controls speed. | Fixed nucleus, finite-distance launches and enlarged markers. No electrons, recoil, quantum or nuclear physics; no cross-section fit. Energy and angular momentum checks include near-centre rays and head-on reversal. Same stopping radii as gravity assist. |
| Resonance bank | Nineteen uncoupled oscillators `x''+2gamma*x'+omega0²*x=0.8 cos(omega*t)`, RK4 dt=1/240. Natural angular frequencies 1…3. | Unit masses, linear springs, positive damping. Cyan curve is analytic steady amplitude; masses show transients as well. Numerical displacement is compared with the phase-correct analytic solution at t=240 across drive/damping endpoints. |
| Flocking | 96 agents: separation within 0.8, alignment/cohesion within 2.5; periodic 16×10 domain, simultaneous updates at dt=1/60. Speed 0.6…2.4; steering acceleration capped at 3. | Behavioural steering inspired by Reynolds, not bird physics. No obstacles or physical collision response. Order is speed-weighted directional alignment. Ballistic wrapping, replay, speed/position limits and order range are tested. |
| Galaxy encounter | Two equal mass-8 cores, 192 massless disk tracers; `G=1`, softened potential `-M/sqrt(r²+0.4²)`, velocity Verlet dt=1/960. Tracers feel both cores. | Restricted planar model. No star self-gravity, gas, dark-matter halos, friction or merger prescription. Initial disk speeds balance their own core only. Tests cover core energy/angular momentum/centre of mass and tracer finiteness over 30 model-time units, including near head-on passes. The energy readout covers the cores only, scaled by initial kinetic plus absolute potential energy so it remains meaningful near zero total energy. |

Equation references: [Lorenz's original paper](https://journals.ametsoc.org/doi/abs/10.1175/1520-0469%281963%29020%3C0130%3Adnf%3E2.0.CO%3B2), [MIT's Gray–Scott formulation](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mac/projects/amorphous/GrayScott/), [NASA's circular restricted three-body formulation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210018983/downloads/Multi_Objective_Reinforcement_Learning_for_Low_Thrust_Transfer_Design_between_Libration_Point_Orbits.pdf), and [MIT's charged-particle lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/22-611j-introduction-to-plasma-physics-i-fall-2006/resources/chap2/). The steering exhibit follows the three local behaviours described by [Craig Reynolds](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/); the restricted encounter is inspired by [Toomre and Toomre's galactic bridges and tails](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/to03000u.html), without claiming to reproduce that paper's fixtures.

## Reading results

Use a result only for the tested fixture, settings and revision. Record units, reference, tolerance and elapsed simulation time. Separate functional failures from slow performance samples and hang-guard timeouts. A native Unity/GPU run is needed for shader output claims; CPU calculations alone do not verify the GPU. Improving a label does not resolve a separate numerical or mechanics issue.
