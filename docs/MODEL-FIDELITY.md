# Models and limits

Fog of War is physics-based torch-ship flight using astronomical data, with idealized spacecraft systems and educational exhibits. True scale means distances and body sizes use kilometres; it does not mean every orbit, surface, image or arrival is an exact reconstruction.

The equations can be exact within a chosen model while that model omits real effects. Numerical error, model error and an unmeasured result are different things. The checks below are implementation checks, not scientific certification. See the [developer guide](DEVELOPING.md#unattended-test-runs) for commands; an unavailable or unrun check supplies no accuracy evidence.

## Spacecraft decisions

These assumptions are retained. Changing one requires a separate mechanics change with planner, economy, cargo and low/zero-fuel checks.

- **Acceleration regulation:** the selected g level commands acceleration, not a fixed force divided by current mass. The drive is assumed to have sufficient thrust reserve for the ship's current dry mass, cargo and propellant. More cargo does not reduce commanded acceleration; it increases the force required and the propellant needed for a given delta-v. As propellant is spent, required force decreases. Fuel follows the ideal rocket equation with the current total mass.
- **Shared RCS technology:** translational RCS deliberately uses the same effective exhaust velocity as the torch drive, `10,000 km/s`. Its acceleration limit is `0.1 g`. Main thrust and RCS both consume reaction mass; the model is not a chemical-RCS system.
- **Electric attitude control:** rotation uses reaction wheels and consumes no reaction mass. Fixed angular acceleration and rate limits remain `1.5 rad/s²` and `0.6 rad/s`. Wheel momentum capacity, saturation/desaturation, electrical draw and cargo-dependent moments of inertia are omitted. A dry ship can still turn.
- **Power and heat:** reactor power, exhaust energy supply, radiator temperature/area and waste-heat rejection impose no limits in the game. The exhaust-power readout uses the ideal nonrelativistic relation `P = ½ m a vₑ`, with mass in kg, applied main-drive and RCS acceleration in m/s² and exhaust velocity in m/s, displayed in TW. This is exhaust kinetic power, not onboard waste heat; the latter needs an efficiency and energy-partition model that this game does not supply. No engine feasibility or numerical waste-heat claim is made. Combat Phase 3 adds one gameplay heat rule, not an engineering claim. A hull with stowable radiators puts a 2 MW waste-heat load into its reaction-mass water (4,186 J/kg·K) for the 2 h after a loud moment and during a raider's chase. The water may warm 100 K. Water that leaves takes its heat with it. A port empties the sink. Drive waste heat is taken to leave with the exhaust. A chased hull that stays dark for that hold, out of the raider's stowed reach, loses it (combat Phase 3b).
- **Signatures (detection):** a ship's signature is that ideal exhaust power (main drive and RCS) plus the hull's cold power: 2 MW with radiators out, ρ² × 2 MW stowed (ρ = 0.3: 0.18 MW, 3,000 km). A sensor sees it to `10,000 km × √(P / 2 MW)` (stock sensors; long-range ×2). This is a detection rule, not a waste-heat model. A raider that sees a burn keeps the track for one boarding time (2 h), or ρ × 2 h while stowable radiators can stow. This is a stale-fix rule (10,000 km / 2 h = 1.389 km/s), a game value. Light delay is omitted: at 0.23 AU it is about 2 minutes. A traffic ship's mass is 500 t × (L / 52 m)³ [design], and a rail ship's thrust is its path acceleration (the Rail leaves out gravity), so its signature fades toward mid-trip. All values are gameplay choices (combat Phases 2 and 3).
- **High g:** sustained crew tolerance is fictionalized; passenger contracts enforce a gameplay comfort limit. No human physiological simulation is claimed.

## Reading results

Use a result only for the tested fixture, settings and revision. Record units, reference, tolerance and elapsed simulation time. Separate functional failures from slow performance samples and hang-guard timeouts. A native Unity/GPU run is needed for shader output claims; CPU calculations alone do not verify the GPU. Improving a label does not resolve a separate numerical or mechanics issue.

## Arrival and contact policy

Airless bodies use local surface-relative contact speed. Contact at up to 20 m/s is supported; faster contact destroys the career ship and reloads its save. Sandbox mode explicitly forgives impacts. Landed position and attitude remain fixed relative to the body's modeled rotation, while the pilot can turn; departure inherits its translational and rotational velocity.

Earth, Venus, Mars and Titan retain an assisted surface-arrival abstraction. Gas/ice giants retain assisted cloud-top hold, never a physical ground landing. The same 20 m/s contact rule applies at the model boundary; it is a game arrival constraint, not a pressure or heating calculation. Assistance holds the contact location without consuming fuel. An outward burn or RCS command releases it. There is no atmospheric-flight regime hidden behind the warning.

Assisted arrival counts as docking only at an existing port on that body, using the same contract-completion and autosave rules as airless contact. No new giant ports are added. Unsafe contact uses the existing destruction/reload recovery; dry ships retain the existing tender/port fuel options. Save/load keeps a body-fixed contact point and the saved orientation; older saves derive that point at their saved epoch and adopt the surface velocity. Time compression follows the body analytically while supported and does not waive impact handling during approach.
